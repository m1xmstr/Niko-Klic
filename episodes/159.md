# Can You Fix the Scrambled Kite?

Klic opens the mail and finds three pieces of a kite postcard. Each piece travelled in a packet with a number. The pieces arrive **3, 1, 2**. Which one belongs at the top?

## Make the postcard

Draw a big kite on a piece of paper. Mark the top strip **1**, the middle **2** and the bottom **3**. Fold the paper along the strip lines and tear it into three pieces.

Give your partner the strips in arrival order **3, 1, 2**. Have them point to the piece they would place first. Assemble the picture and check the edges. Then hide strip 2 and deliver the other two. What single number should your partner ask for?

## Try the puzzle in Python

This small example shows the job our postcard program does after delivery.

```python
def unpack(delivery):
    slots = dict(delivery)
    return [slots.get(number, "missing") for number in (1, 2, 3)]

print(unpack([(3, "tail"), (1, "nose"), (2, "wings")]))
print(unpack([(3, "tail"), (1, "nose")]))
```

The first result is `['nose', 'wings', 'tail']`. The second is `['nose', 'missing', 'tail']`.

In the episode, one program opens two places on the same computer to send and receive messages. These are called sockets. The sender chooses the order. The receiver reads the numbers, finds the gap and asks for the missing piece. Ask your partner what would happen if you shuffled all three again but kept their numbers.
