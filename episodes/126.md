# Episode 126 — Can We Trust That Answer? AI Fact-Checking

**Status:** Published October 23, 2026; public playback and manual English captions verified.

[Watch Episode 126](https://youtube.com/shorts/Xca9Nk3nElo).

**Track:** AI literacy. **Prerequisites:** [121 — AI](121.md) and [123 — LLMs](123.md).

## Learning goal
Pause before accepting a confident AI answer. With a grown-up, find a source suited to the question, compare the claim with what it actually says, and correct the answer or keep checking.

## Story
Nico sees a very sure-sounding answer about the Moon. Klic holds back the check stamp while Nico opens the fact book. They compare the claim with NASA's Moonlight article, pause for a prediction, then correct the claim before stamping the comparison slip.

The incorrect answer is **scripted fiction**, not a captured response from an AI service. The animated book and cards are original illustrations, not a live website or a NASA endorsement. No code or model execution is claimed.

## Check the example

| Step | Claim | Result of source comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pretend answer | The Moon makes its own light. | Contradicted by the source. |
| Corrected answer | The Moon reflects sunlight. | Supported by the source. |

[NASA Science: Moonlight](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moonlight/) explains that the visible light we see from the Moon is reflected sunlight. The relevant passage was checked on October 18, 2026. NASA is an appropriate source here because it studies the Moon; a source's relevance and evidence matter, rather than a confident voice or polished appearance.

This small example does not mean every AI answer is wrong, or that any single source settles every question. For a simple fact, read the relevant passage in context. If sources disagree or the answer remains unclear, ask a knowledgeable grown-up and keep checking.

## Predict, reveal, and change
1. Read the pretend claim and the source fact aloud.
2. Pause for two silent seconds: do they agree?
3. Reveal: no. Replace the claim with the corrected sentence.
4. Compare again: the corrected sentence matches the source.

## Grown-up activity
Use two paper cards, labeled **CLAIM** and **SOURCE**. Choose one simple, non-personal science claim, such as the example above. A grown-up opens the source and helps read its relevant passage. The child places a question mark beside an unchecked claim, then marks whether it is supported, contradicted, or still uncertain. Rewrite a contradicted claim and compare it again. Keep the source title beside the corrected card.

No child account or personal information is needed. Use fictional examples; do not paste private details into an AI service.

## Check understanding
- **Does sounding certain prove an answer is true?** No. Compare the claim with relevant evidence.
- **What changed in our example?** The answer changed from making light to reflecting sunlight.
- **Why did Klic wait before stamping?** The claim had not yet been checked.
- **What if you cannot find a clear source?** Keep the answer marked uncertain and ask a grown-up to help.

**Recap:** Pause, find a source, compare, and correct. Don't just guess.
