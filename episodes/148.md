# Episode 148 — Two Times. Can We Choose?

[Watch Episode 148](https://youtube.com/shorts/G6Blp2UmQqI)

Nico wants to plan a pretend moon show. Two fictional notes give different opening times. Neither note says it is the current schedule. A helpful answer should preserve that uncertainty instead of silently choosing a time.

## Read both clues with a grown-up

| Fictional note | Opening time | Marked current? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 10 AM | Not given |
| B | 11 AM | Not given |

Before revealing an answer, ask: Can we responsibly choose ten, choose eleven, or say we cannot tell from these notes?

Our **scripted answer example** is: “Cannot tell from these notes.” Explain that the two notes disagree, and ask for a current authoritative schedule. Neither note is automatically preferred just because it appears first or last.

## Change one clue

Change only Note B’s time to **10 AM**. Leave the currentness field unspecified on both notes. What do they support now?

Our scripted answer is: “Both notes say 10 AM.” This accurately reports their contents. It does **not** verify the real opening time: the notes might both be outdated or copy the same error. Our pretend plan now states its source and retains the need for a current schedule.

## What was actually checked

The animation uses invented source cards and scripted answer examples. **No AI model was called to generate these answers.** A local Python check verified the differing times, the single changed field, and the displayed answers; a fresh Python process repeated the changed case. This is a consistency check on a teaching example, not proof that a model always handles conflicts correctly or that a real show opens at ten.

A small optional Python exercise:

```python
notes = [
    {"note": "A", "opens": "10 AM", "current": None},
    {"note": "B", "opens": "11 AM", "current": None},
]

def describe(notes):
    times = {note["opens"] for note in notes}
    if len(times) != 1:
        return "Cannot tell from these notes"
    return "Both notes say " + next(iter(times))

print(describe(notes))
notes[1]["opens"] = "10 AM"
print(describe(notes))
```

Expected output:

```text
Cannot tell from these notes
Both notes say 10 AM
```

The code deliberately does not decide which source is current or authoritative. It only compares the supplied strings. No network access, account, personal data, paid service, or physical equipment is involved.

## Paper activity

Make two pretend notes about a fictional event. First give them conflicting times. Practice an answer that names the conflict and asks what evidence would resolve it. Then make the times match and practice saying both what the notes support and what remains unverified. Ask whether the two notes are independent or could share an error.

Prerequisite: [Episode 138 — missing evidence](138.md). Missing evidence and conflicting evidence are different reasons to avoid an unsupported definite answer.
