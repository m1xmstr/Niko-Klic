# When Should AI Say Unknown? — grown-up guide

Scheduled November 2, 2026 at 3PM America/Chicago; public playback verification pending.

Episode 138; backlog idea 445. AI literacy: distinguish a missing fact from a supported answer. Prerequisites: lessons 126 and 128.

Nico wants to plan a pretend exhibit visit. Klic notices that the first source card gives a room but no opening time. An appropriate answer is “Unknown from this note.” It would be unsupported to invent ten o’clock. A second card adds that exact time. The changed-case question stays unanswered while viewers inspect the new evidence.

## Fictional source cards

Original:

```text
Moon exhibit
Room 2
```

Updated:

```text
Moon exhibit
Room 2
Opens: 10 AM
```

**Question:** What time does it open? From the original card, the time is unknown. From the updated card, the supported answer is 10 AM. The new fact changes what the evidence supports.

These are fictional cards and scripted answer examples, not recorded AI responses or claims about a real exhibit. The local production check verifies that the opening-time field is absent from the first card and present in the second; it does not test an AI model. The video does not imply that every AI system reliably admits uncertainty.

## Try together

With a trusted grown-up, write a fictional invitation containing a place but no time. Ask what is known and what is missing. Add a time on a second card. Predict the answer before uncovering it, then point to the exact supporting words. Never use a real address, personal schedule or account details.

If two sources disagree, adding more text alone does not settle which is reliable. This lesson demonstrates a missing fact supplied by a fictional updated source; checking source reliability is a separate step.
